    How the numbers are worked out

    • Stress is σ = F/A. With a spring rate of 0 the force stays constant. With a spring rate k, the wire and spring are in series: the heated section grows by αLhΔT, the spring relaxes, and the tension falls to F = [F₀(1/K_w + 1/k) − αLhΔT] / (1/K_w,hot + 1/k), where K_w = AE/L over the overall wire length and Lh is the heated length between + and −.
    • Cold stiffness (power off): δ = FL/AE and K = F/δ, which equals AE/L.
    • Stiffness, power on: K = A / (Lh/Ehot + (L − Lh)/E). It is the same AE/L as power off, but the heated section uses the lower Young's modulus at the wire temperature. Thermal growth changes the length and the tension, not the stiffness, so it is shown separately as stretch.
    • Resistance: R = ρLh/A over the heated length between + and −, with resistivity rising with temperature. Current, power and voltage use that length. Stiffness and stretch use the overall length. Heat conducted into the unheated ends is ignored.
    • Current at a given wire temperature comes from a heat balance in still air at 25 °C: I²R = natural convection + radiation. The wire loses extra heat into the foam while cutting, so these are idle-wire values.
    • Factor of safety is the chosen strength (tensile or yield) at the wire temperature divided by σ. Strength falls with temperature along an approximate curve for each alloy. Treat hot FOS as a guide and test on the machine.
    • PWM duty is the share of full supply power needed for the target temperature.
